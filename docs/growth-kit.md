# charitybibs.com — launch and growth kit

The honest version. Everything here is posted as you, by name, saying you built it.

## The one thing to understand about timing

Traffic for this product is a spike, not a curve. Search interest for "NYC marathon charity bib" peaks in the days after drawing results (early March) and again as charity deadlines approach (Sept–Oct). You are launching in October 2026 — the tail end of the 2026 season. That is fine. Your job between now and March 2027 is to get the domain **indexed, linked, and aged** so it ranks when the 2027 spike hits.

Do not read October traffic as a verdict. Read March.

- Don't post the same text twice.
- Don't argue with people who say charity bibs are fine. Say "totally — the site isn't against them, it's for reading the terms first."

## Weeks 2–8 — links and corrections

This is the work that makes March happen. Google ranks pages that other pages link to.

### Charities themselves (the best links you can get)

Email every charity whose row is favorable — the ones with lower exposure, published terms, or a deferral path. Template:

> Subject: charitybibs.com lists your NYC Marathon program — please check it
>
> Hi — I built charitybibs.com, a side-by-side comparison of NYC Marathon charity bib terms. Your program is listed. I pulled every field from your public page and linked to it; I'd rather be corrected than wrong, so if anything is off, reply and I'll fix it the same day.
>
> If it's accurate and useful, a link from your marathon page would help runners find the comparison. No cost, no ask beyond that.
>
> Joyce Zhou · joycezhou.me

Charities with unfavorable rows: email them too, same template minus the link ask. Some will ask you to delist them. Decide your policy before that happens — the site's answer should be "we list published terms; if the published terms change, the row changes."

### Running media

Send one email each, with the three most surprising numbers and the link. They write about this in March; you want to be in their bookmarks in October.

- Ashley Mateo (Substack — wrote "why it's so hard to get into the NYC Marathon")
- Canadian Running Magazine (covered the 1% acceptance rate)
- Marathon Handbook
- Runner's World US (long shot)
- Marathon Ballot — they run the broad directory; propose a link swap: they link to you for terms depth, you link to them for ballot dates. They might say no. Ask anyway.

### Where else to be listed

- Marathon Ballot's NYC charity page (ask for a "see also")

### Corrections are marketing

Every correction email you get is someone who cares enough to read closely. Fix it fast, reply personally, log it publicly in a `CHANGELOG.md`. "Corrected within 4 hours" is the reputation that gets the site linked.

## What to measure

Plausible, weekly. Three numbers only.

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Sessions from Reddit / Discord vs. from Google | Whether SEO is starting to work (Google share should climb through winter) |
| Outbound clicks to charity pages / sessions | Whether people act on what they see. Target from the PRD: ≥8% |
| "still taking runners" filter use / sessions | Whether people are here to act now vs. plan for next year — tells you what March needs |

Ignore daily users. Ignore bounce rate. The page is meant to be read once, hard.

## The finding so far (put this in the writeup)

Version 1 of this page sorted by "what you actually owe" — minimum plus entry plus fees. With 27 charities loaded, that column was the headline plus ~$315 for almost everyone, and only 6 rows changed rank. The hypothesis was wrong and the kill criterion in the PRD caught it. Say that first; it's the most credible sentence in the writeup.

What actually varies between charities is not the price. It's:

- **Support.** Support scores run 0 to 8 of 10. Boston Children's, Fred's Team, V Foundation, NAMI-NYC, Make-A-Wish, Tunnel to Towers, Concern, and Achilles run real programs — coaches, group runs, fundraising staff, team events. Circle of Care publishes nothing. Same bib.
- **Whether you can still get in.** By mid-September 2026, 7 of 27 are open, 9 waitlisted, 7 closed. A page that doesn't show status sends people to dead doors.
- **Whether the terms are published.** 12 of 27 publish what happens on a shortfall; 3 publish a free-exit date; 4 publish an injury policy; 3 publish deferral. Fifteen of 27 publish none of the four — you learn the terms in the agreement, after you apply.

And the reframe that came out of it: the barrier to running for charity isn't hidden cost, it's *fear of not raising the money*. The support column is the answer to that fear. The page's job changed from "warn people" to "help people commit to the charity most likely to get them across the line." That's a better product and a better story than the one you started with.

The research also closed a data error: the Team for Kids "$2,200" figure was TFK's Chicago program. NYC is $3,000. Worth mentioning as an example of why every field links to its source.

## Not doing

- Fake reviews, sock puppets, friends posting as strangers — covered above.
- Paid ads — the audience is tiny and the site has no revenue.
- A newsletter — no one wants a newsletter about a once-a-year decision. An email capture for "tell me when 2027 data is posted" is fine (P1 in the PRD).
- Expanding to Boston/Chicago before NYC is right. Depth first.
