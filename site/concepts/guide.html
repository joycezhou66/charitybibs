Didn't get in through the drawing? A charity bib is a guaranteed spot in the race. In return you promise to raise a set amount for the charity, usually $3,000 to $5,000, by a deadline. You give them a credit card when you're accepted. If you don't raise it all, they charge the rest to the card. That's normal; it's how the charity covers the spot it already paid for.

The charities are more different than the prices. Twenty-two of the twenty-seven ask for between $3,000 and $5,000. What varies is the help: some give you a coach, group runs, a fundraising helper, a team, a bus to the start. Some give you a bib and a deadline. That's the difference between the money being easy and being hard to raise.

So read this guide in three passes. First find a cause you'd be proud to ask friends to give to. Then, within it, pick for the help. Then check they're still taking runners and read the fine print, which opens under each charity when you click it.

    The four fine print questions

    1. If I don't raise it all by the deadline, what happens, and when?
    2. If I get hurt and can't run, do I still owe the money?
    3. Can I move my spot to next year?
    4. Until what date can I back out for free?

    If a charity doesn't answer one, ask before you sign up. Any charity with a real program answers these in one email. Keep the reply.

    Where the numbers come from

    Every amount, every "what they give you," and every quote was read from the charity's own web page and links back to it. If something says "they don't say," we couldn't find it published. That's not a no. Ask.

    The cause label is the one thing written by hand. Everything else is the charity's words. The sheet is re-checked every week. Spot a mistake? Tell us and it's fixed within two days.
