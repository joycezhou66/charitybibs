How it works

What a charity bib is

A charity bib is a guaranteed spot in the race. If you didn't get in through the drawing, every official charity partner can still get you in. In return you promise to raise a set amount for the charity, usually $3,000 to $5,000, by a deadline.

You give them a credit card when you're accepted. If you don't raise it all, they charge the rest to the card. That's normal. It's how the charity covers the spot it already paid for.

The charities are more different than the prices. Some give you a coach, group runs, a fundraising helper, a team, a bus to the start. Some give you a bib and a deadline. That's the difference between the money being easy and being hard to raise, and it's the main thing this site shows.

The four fine print questions

Every charity's fine print on this site counts how many of these four questions the charity answers on its own pages:

  1. If I don't raise it all by the deadline, what happens, and when?
  2. If I get hurt and can't run, do I still owe the money?
  3. Can I move my spot to next year?
  4. Until what date can I back out for free?

If a charity doesn't answer one, ask before you sign up. Any charity with a real program answers these in one email. Keep the reply.

Where the numbers come from

Every amount, every "what they give you," and every quote was read from the charity's own web page and links back to it. Each charity's fine print shows the date it was last read.

If something says "they don't say," it means we couldn't find it published. That's not a no. Ask.

The cause label is the one thing written by hand, so the list is easy to scan. Everything else is the charity's words. The site is re-checked every week against the charities' pages, and nothing is estimated.
